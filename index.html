How I'd structure Meta, Google, and Amazon for Typical.

This is a working draft of where I'd take performance and growth, and the thinking behind it.

What I'm seeing

Us

A young brand with a real head start

Four months old, strong design, and a founder who has built a category brand before. The product isn't the problem. The problem is that almost nobody is looking for us yet, and the launch attention has a shelf life.

The category

Demand with no brand attached

Ask people their favourite towel brand and they stall. On Amazon the category is mostly commodity listings with no point of view. That gap is the opportunity: there's real buying intent sitting there unclaimed.

Competitors

Everyone leads with bedding

The credible home brands treat towels as a side line, so they under-invest in the exact place we're strongest. Legacy luxury feels old and is priced out of reach. Value players win on price but have nothing to say.

So here's the approach, and why

The three paid platforms do different jobs, and the plan runs them as one funnel rather than three separate accounts. Meta and TikTok build demand we don't have yet. Google and Amazon capture the demand that already exists when someone goes looking. Treating Amazon as a first-class channel matters, because a large share of towel buying starts there, and a design brand that ignores it hands that search to commodity sellers. Early on I'd weight money toward the top of the funnel to build reach and an owned list while the category is still open, and I'd judge all of it on contribution margin and payback rather than ROAS alone, since a towel is durable and we can't lean on people reordering.

Creative direction, conversion, and audiences.

On a performance team the creative is the biggest lever, and the page it lands on is the next — so this is where a lot of the early wins are. Below: the creative direction I'd set and the loop that sharpens it, then the on-site and Amazon test backlog scored by impact, and the frameworks for creative and audiences.

Creative direction

Performance creative does most of the targeting and most of the persuading, so I'd own the creative direction on paid — the angle, the brief, and what "on-brand and on-message" means — working hand in hand with the creative team rather than over them. Here's the point of view I'd bring and the loop that keeps it improving.

The direction

Sell the feeling, lead with the design

Towels are bought on feel and on looks. I'd push creative that makes the softness and the design point of view almost tactile — the stretch as the scroll-stopper, colour and texture as the hook — and keep every cut unmistakably Typical, so the ads build the brand even while they sell a set.

Who owns what

I set the brief, the team makes it sing

I'd own the angle, the order of messages, and which concepts get budget; the creative team owns craft and execution. Every concept ships with a tight brief — the job, the audience, the one thing it has to land — so we brief on intent rather than art-direct by committee.

Closing the loop

The numbers write the next brief

Each test feeds back: which hook held attention, which angle converted, which format earned its cost. That becomes a short, specific brief for the next batch, so the team starts each round ahead of the last. Winners get scaled; losers get documented, not repeated.

The creative feedback loop

A tight weekly loop between performance and creative is the engine the role is really asking for. It keeps the team close to what's working and turns the ad account into a steady source of creative learning, not just a place assets go to be spent.

1 · Watch
Read the signal
Hook rate, hold, cost per result, and which angle actually converts — by concept, not just by campaign.
2 · Distil
Pull the insight
Turn the numbers into one or two clear learnings: what to keep, what to drop, what to try next.
3 · Brief
Direct the next batch
A short brief per concept — the job, the audience, the single message, a reference — handed to the team.
4 · Make
The team creates
Creative builds the variants, changing one deliberate lever at a time so the read stays clean.
5 · Test
Ship & learn
Launch the batch, scale winners, document losers, and the read kicks off the loop again next week.

Creative testing framework

Test one variable at a time, in batches, on a weekly cadence

The point is to learn what actually drives performance, not to flood the account with random assets. Each week a batch of variants goes live changing a single lever, the winners get scaled and the losers documented, and the learnings go back to the creative team as a short brief so the next batch starts ahead of where the last one did. Over a quarter that compounds into a real library of what works for this brand.

Audience building

Prospecting

Start broad and let the platform find buyers, with interest and lookalike sets as a structured test against broad. Layer in design, home, and gifting affinities. On Amazon, in-market and category audiences. The creative does most of the targeting work.

Retargeting

Segment by depth: viewed a product, added to cart, started checkout, each with its own message and urgency. Engaged-but-didn't-buy from social. Amazon DSP for viewers once volume justifies it.

Owned & lookalikes

Feed purchaser and high-LTV lists back as seed audiences, and suppress recent buyers from prospecting so we're not paying to reach people who just bought. Build a list for the next drop now.

Email & SMS — the owned channel.

For a product people don't reorder often, the owned list is where retention actually lives, and it's the fastest payback in the plan. It does three jobs: automated flows that earn around the clock, one-time campaigns that carry the brand and the drops, and the segmentation underneath that makes both land. Built in Klaviyo and Postscript, and wired to suppress and feed paid rather than run beside it.

Campaigns & segmentation

One-time sends · campaigns

Carry the brand between the drops

Flows earn quietly; campaigns set the tempo. A planned, value-led calendar — segmented, never a blast to the whole list — so it stays warm and engaged instead of trained to tune out.

  • New colour and product drops — early-access list first, then the full file
  • Gifting moments year-round — holidays, weddings, housewarming, Mother's & Father's Day
  • Editorial & brand — the design point of view, care guides, behind-the-scenes
  • Value done right — set and bundle offers, not sitewide discounts that train people to wait
  • Restocks, limited runs, and back-in-stock moments
  • Social proof — reviews, press, and real customer homes
Segmentation · the layer underneath

Right message, right list, right channel

Both flows and campaigns only work if they're targeted. I'd segment on lifecycle, behaviour, value, and engagement — and protect deliverability by sending mainly to people who actually open.

  • Lifecycle — non-buyer, one-time, repeat, lapsed, VIP / high-LTV
  • Behaviour — category and colour interest, set vs single, gifting buyers
  • Value — predicted LTV and AOV tiers for VIP treatment and discount suppression
  • Engagement — 30 / 60 / 90-day actives; sunset the dormant to protect sender reputation
  • Consent & channel — SMS-opted vs email-only, so each message runs on the right rail

Model assumptions

Base scenario: $14 CPM for prospecting reach; blended CAC $40 on prospecting + retargeting + conversion spend; AOV $82; gross margin 62%, so roughly $50.80 contribution per order before acquisition cost; retention spend excluded from new-customer math. Conservative widens CAC and softens ROAS by about 15%; Aggressive tightens them by about 15%. The rest of the mix.

Channels and plays that sit alongside the core three: where they fit, and how I'd sequence them so they add rather than distract.

TikTok Ads

Where the product gets to show off

The stretch is the kind of thing that stops a scroll, and TikTok rewards native, creator-made video over polished ads. I'd start with Spark Ads boosting organic and creator posts that already perform, keep budget modest until something earns more, and pull the winners into the wider paid mix. It doubles as a cheap creative testing ground for hooks I can reuse on Meta.

  • Spark Ads on top organic and creator content first
  • Treat it as a hook-testing lab, not just a spend line
  • Scale only what beats Meta on cost per result
Influencer & affiliate

Seed, then whitelist, then pay on performance

Send product to people whose taste genuinely fits the brand and see who posts unprompted. The ones who perform get whitelisted so their content runs as paid from their handle, which usually beats brand-account ads. A simple affiliate program gives them a reason to keep posting, and keeps the cost tied to actual sales rather than flat fees.

  • Gifting list built around design and home creators
  • Whitelisting the top performers into paid social
  • Affiliate codes so spend follows results
Email & SMS · Klaviyo and Postscript

The fastest payback in the plan

My read is the brand captures emails well but the automated flows are thin, which is usually money sitting on the table. The flows below come first because they earn while everything else is still being built, and they're where retention actually lives for a product people don't reorder often.

  • Welcome series that earns the signup in the brand's voice
  • Abandoned browse, cart, and checkout
  • Post-purchase care and cross-sell to the full set
  • Win-back and lapsed-buyer
  • Early access for the next drop, building the launch list
New product launch workflow

A repeatable way to launch the next colour, set, or category

Typical will keep dropping new things, so the launch should run on a process rather than a scramble. This is the sequence I'd document and reuse, tightening it each time based on what the last launch taught us.

1 · Tease
Build the list
Waitlist and early-access signups, organic teasers, creator seeding before anything's for sale.
2 · Prime
Warm the audience
Email and SMS to the list, PR and creator content landing, retargeting set up and waiting.
3 · Launch
Open to warm demand
List goes first, paid switches on across Meta, Google, Amazon, listings and PDPs ready to convert.
4 · Amplify
Scale what works
Push budget to the best creative and channels, whitelist top creator posts, watch contribution margin.
5 · Sustain
Retarget & restock
Convert the considerers, manage stock with ops, fold the drop into evergreen and the next launch list.
SEO fundamentals

Quiet, compounding, worth starting day one

Two surfaces. On Shopify, fix indexation, build category content that answers what people actually search before they know the brand, and earn the term over time. On Amazon, get titles, bullets, A+ content, and backend keywords right so listings rank and convert on-platform. Neither pays off this quarter, which is exactly why I'd start now rather than later.
