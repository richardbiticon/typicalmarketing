The five-minute version.

A talk track to say in your own words, not a script to memorise. The large text is roughly what you say; the grey notes say what to click or point at. Most of the time should sit on the first 90 days, since that's the part the founders will judge. Aim for an unhurried pace and leave gaps to breathe.

0:00–0:35OpenStart on the approach

"The most useful thing I can tell you about Typical is that the towel is where we start, but it's probably not where the value is. You picked a category nobody has bothered to brand. Ask anyone their favourite towel brand and they go blank. That blank is the whole opportunity, and it sits over most of the home, not just the bathroom."

"So the way I see my job: get us to be the name people think of in a category that doesn't have one yet, before someone with more money notices the same gap."

Note  Open on the approach page. Say the first line slowly and let it sit before you carry on.
0:35–1:20The catchApproach · the two-lens toggle

"There's one fact that shapes everything. A towel lasts for years. People don't reorder it the way they reorder shampoo, so the usual playbook of making your money back on the second purchase doesn't apply. Run the business that way and the numbers look small, and you end up afraid to spend."

"Measured properly, it's a different business. We grow on how much someone buys at once, and on how many of your categories they buy into as you add them. Same company, much bigger ceiling."

Note  Flip the two-lens toggle live. The switch makes the point quicker than you can say it.
1:20–2:05How we growApproach · the three cards + map

"Growth comes from three places, then. Winning new customers, getting them to buy bigger baskets with sets, and adding categories and countries that re-sell to the audience we already paid for. Gifting runs underneath all of it, because a beautiful towel is one of the easiest gifts to buy, and every gift puts us in a new home for free."

"And the lane really is open. Every home brand leads with bedding and treats towels as an afterthought. Towel-first, with actual taste, is a spot nobody's standing in."

Note  Click through the three cards, then the positioning map. Point at the empty corner.
2:05–3:45The first 90 days — spend the most time hereSwitch to the 90-day plan

"Here's how I'd turn that into action in the first quarter."

Days 0–30
Diagnose
Get access, sit with you and the creative team, audit every channel including Amazon, and get the reporting onto contribution margin and payback rather than just ROAS. Fix the obvious leaks: the cart, uncovered brand search, weak Amazon listings.
Days 31–60
Build
Build the dashboards and reporting, rebuild the Klaviyo and Postscript flows, set up a creative testing system with the team, and launch structured tests across Meta, Google, Amazon, and TikTok on clean tracking.
Days 61–90
Scale
Move budget toward what's working on contribution margin, set pacing rules, and write down a repeatable way to launch the next colour, category, or country so it doesn't depend on me being in the room.

"The logic is simple. I won't scale spend until I trust the numbers, so the first month is mostly listening and fixing. Then build the systems, since there's no playbook to inherit. Then put money behind what works. By day 90 you've got a working engine and a way to repeat it, not a pile of experiments."

Note  This is the centre of the pitch. Move to the 90-day plan, open the roadmap, click each phase as you talk. Slow down here.
3:45–4:30What good looks like90-day plan · budget & reporting

"What does progress look like? Acquisition cost coming down, average order going up through sets, and email and SMS growing from under a fifth of revenue toward a third. I'd weight the budget toward the top of the funnel early, because building reach and an owned list is the priority while the category is still open."

"And there's a clock on it. The window to own this category is maybe twelve to eighteen months, so I'd want to move now rather than wait for everything to be perfectly proven."

Note  Drag a slider on the budget tool so the figures move while you talk. One live moment beats a static chart.
4:30–5:00CloseBack to the approach · the ask

"So in plain terms: I'd treat this as building a brand and a portfolio, not as selling towels more efficiently, and the 90-day plan is how I'd start."

"What I'd want from you is agreement on that, and a budget set aside for the first tests. Give me a quarter and I'll come back with an honest read on the numbers, channels that work, and a way to launch the next product without me in the room."

Note  State the ask plainly, then stop. A little silence after the ask is good.

A few delivery notes

  • Have both pages open before you start. The links along the top of each move between them in one click.
  • Lead with how you read the business, not the channel list. The channels are the proof, not the pitch.
  • The 90-day beat is the spine. If you get cut short, drop the "what good looks like" beat before you touch it.
  • Let the toggles and the budget slider carry the visuals while you talk over them.
  • Be upfront that the numbers are placeholders until week one. The founders will trust the person who says so.
  • End on the ask. A pitch with no clear request is just a presentation.

A talk track to adapt in your own voice, drawn from the approach and the 90-day plan. The figures are illustrative placeholders to be replaced with Typical's real data, and the timings are guides, so rehearse it once out loud and adjust to your own pace.
